# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Anyone who wants to understand how the prices of everyday products have changed over time. Not a financial professional — an ordinary person checking whether that bag of rice costs more than it did six months ago. They visit occasionally, pick a product, and read the chart. Low cognitive investment expected per session.

## Product Purpose

A personal inflation tracker that makes real price history visible and readable. Users search for a product and see its price trend over time. Success means someone leaves the app with a clear sense of whether something got cheaper or more expensive — and by roughly how much.

The app has a public-facing surface (landing page or about page) in addition to the product itself.

## Brand Personality

Clear, honest, grounded. No hype, no gamification, no emotional manipulation around money. The tone is that of a calm friend who looked up the data for you and is showing you a clean chart.

## Anti-references

- **Crypto dashboards**: neon on black, ticker overload, manufactured urgency.
- **Generic SaaS templates**: gradient hero, feature grid, social proof row, "Start for free" CTAs on every fold.
- **Colorful finance apps (Mint, YNAB)**: overly bright palettes, budget-tracker aesthetic, gamified progress rings.

## Design Principles

1. **Data first.** The chart is the product. UI chrome frames data and steps aside; it never competes for attention.
2. **Trust through legibility.** Every number is readable, every label is honest. No visual tricks that make a trend look more dramatic than it is.
3. **Quiet confidence.** Nothing needs to impress. Minimal chrome, comfortable density, things just work without ceremony.
4. **Accessible to everyone.** Inflation affects everyone regardless of financial literacy. Navigation and labels should require zero domain knowledge.
5. **Show, don't explain.** A clear chart beats a paragraph describing it. Copy should answer "what happened" not "how our product works."

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Charts must have accessible alternatives (screen reader labels, color-independent encoding). Portuguese as primary language. No jargon.
